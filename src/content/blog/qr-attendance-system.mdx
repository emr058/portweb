## Why Manual Attendance Fails

Every classroom session starts with 10–15 minutes of roll call. Paper sign-in sheets get lost. Manual counts have errors. And nobody analyzes the data.

The business case was clear: **automate attendance, get accurate data, save time**.

## Two Modes, One System

I built a dual-mode system to handle different environments:

### Mode 1: QR Code Check-in
- Admin generates session-specific QR codes
- Students scan with any smartphone camera
- Instant check-in with timestamp and device fingerprint

### Mode 2: ESP32 Face Recognition
- ESP32-CAM module at classroom entrance
- Optimized PyTorch model running on-device
- Contactless check-in for high-traffic scenarios

Both modes feed into the same backend API and dashboard.

## Edge AI Challenges

Running face recognition on ESP32 was the hardest technical challenge:

- Full PyTorch models don't fit in 520KB RAM
- Inference must complete in under 2 seconds
- Lighting conditions vary wildly in classroom environments

**Solution**: Model quantization (INT8), face detection with MTCNN on-device, and embedding comparison against a pre-computed database rather than full inference per frame.

## Handling Scale

During class transitions, 500+ students need to check in within 5 minutes. That's ~1.7 check-ins per second.

The backend uses:
- **WebSocket connections** for real-time updates
- **Redis pub/sub** for broadcasting attendance events
- **Batch writes** to PostgreSQL to avoid connection pool exhaustion

## Results

| Metric | Before | After |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Check-in time | 15 min | 30 sec |
| Recognition accuracy | Manual | 96.2% |
| Record accuracy | ~85% | 99.8% |

## Lessons Learned

1. **Always provide a fallback** — QR scan when face recognition fails
2. **Audit trails matter** — log who checked in, when, and via which method
3. **Edge AI needs optimization** — don't assume cloud models work on microcontrollers

## Future Plans

Geofencing to prevent remote check-in, LMS integration, and predictive analytics for at-risk students.
