## Starting With the Problem, Not the Tech

My first instinct was to build features. Notification scheduling, adherence tracking, analytics dashboards — the engineering possibilities were endless.

But the first lesson hit hard: **nobody cares about your tech stack**. They care about whether their problem gets solved.

For B12 Reminder, the problem was simple: people forget to take their supplements. The solution needed to be even simpler.

## Validation Before Code

Before writing a single line of code, I:

1. Talked to 15 people on B12 regimens
2. Asked about their current reminder methods (phone alarms, sticky notes, nothing)
3. Identified the #1 pain point: **alarm fatigue** — they disable reminders after a week

This shaped the entire product: smart scheduling that adapts, not fixed daily alarms.

## Technical Decisions That Mattered

### PWA Over Native App
For MVP, a Progressive Web App beat native development:
- No app store approval delays
- Push notifications via Web Push API
- Single codebase for all platforms
- Instant updates without user action

### Timezone-Aware Cron Jobs
Supplement schedules cross time zones when users travel. The reminder engine stores schedules in UTC and converts at notification time, not at schedule creation.

### Adherence Without Guilt
Most health apps use streaks. Research shows streaks create anxiety when broken. B12 Reminder uses **weekly compliance percentages** instead — motivating without punishing.

## What I'd Do Differently

1. **Launch earlier** — I spent 3 weeks on UI polish that users didn't notice
2. **Charge from day one** — free users give different feedback than paying users
3. **Build analytics first** — understanding usage patterns should precede feature development

## Metrics That Matter for SaaS

Don't track vanity metrics. Track:

- **Activation rate**: % of signups who set their first reminder
- **Week-1 retention**: % who still use the app after 7 days
- **Adherence improvement**: the actual health outcome you're selling

## The Biggest Lesson

Building SaaS is 30% engineering and 70% understanding your user. The best code in the world doesn't matter if you're solving the wrong problem.

Start with the problem. Measure the outcome. Then build.
